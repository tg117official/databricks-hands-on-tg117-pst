1. Why model data before building Gold?

An e-commerce platform records customers, products, orders, payments, promotions, inventory, shipments, and refunds. Storing these facts without a model may allow the application to run, but it can also create duplicated customers, missing relationships, inconsistent statuses, and incorrect revenue reports.

A data model is an agreed structure for business information. It defines what must be stored, how records are identified, how business objects relate, and which rules keep the data trustworthy. When these decisions are clear, engineers can build pipelines and Gold tables that preserve business meaning instead of merely moving columns.

EcomSphere example: an order may contain several items and have several payment attempts. If those two relationships are combined without understanding their row meaning, the same sale can appear multiple times and inflate revenue.

Lesson outcomes

  • Identify entities, attributes, records, domains, and table grain.
  • Use primary keys and foreign keys to protect business relationships.
  • Read one-to-one, one-to-many, and many-to-many ER diagrams.
  • Use degree, cardinality, ordinality, and participation constraints correctly.
  • Distinguish conceptual, logical, and physical data models.
  • Explain why operational and analytical models serve different workloads.

3. Entities, attributes, records, and domains

An entity is a person, object, event, or concept that matters to the business. Customer, Product, Order, Payment, and Promotion are EcomSphere entities. A database table is the physical structure used to store records about an entity.

An attribute describes an entity. A customer's name, email, country, and status are attributes; they become columns in the physical table. A record is one occurrence of the entity, such as customer 101, Aditi Sharma.

A domain defines the allowed kind or range of values. EcomSphere stores customer status in a controlled lookup table, because mixing ACTIVE, Active, and ACTV would split one business category across reports and operational rules.

Entity

Business object or event: Customer, Order, Payment.

Attribute

Descriptive characteristic: email, order date, amount.

Record

One stored occurrence: order 1001 placed by customer 101.

Entity type, entity instance, and entity set

Customer is an entity type because it describes a kind of business object. Aditi Sharma is one entity instance. All customer records stored at a given time form the entity set. In MySQL, these ideas appear as a table definition, one row, and the table's current rows.

Modelling termPlain meaningMySQL view
Entity typeThe kind of thing being describedTable definition
Entity instanceOne real exampleOne row
Entity setAll current examples of that typeCurrent table rows
AttributeOne detail about the entityColumn

4. One row must have one clear meaning

The meaning of one row is the table's grain. In orders, one row represents one order. In order_item, one row represents one product variant included in an order. Order 1001 therefore appears once in orders but twice in order_item.

Grain is more than a definition: it controls safe calculations. Summing an order-level total after joining it to item-level rows repeats the total once for every item, which can make a dashboard report revenue that the business never earned.

Demonstration - Observe two different table grains 
SELECT 'orders' AS table_name,
       COUNT(*) AS row_count,
       'one row per order' AS row_meaning
FROM orders
UNION ALL
SELECT 'order_item',
       COUNT(*),
       'one row per order item'
FROM order_item;

SELECT
    o.order_id,
    o.grand_total,
    oi.order_item_id,
    oi.variant_id,
    oi.line_total
FROM orders AS o
JOIN order_item AS oi
  ON o.order_id = oi.order_id
ORDER BY o.order_id, oi.order_item_id;
Observe: there are 3 order rows and 4 order-item rows. Order 1001 repeats because it contains two items.
Demonstration - See how unclear grain inflates a total 
-- Incorrect: each order total repeats for every matching item.
SELECT SUM(o.grand_total) AS incorrect_total
FROM orders AS o
JOIN order_item AS oi
  ON o.order_id = oi.order_id;

-- Correct for this order-level measure: sum the orders table once.
SELECT SUM(grand_total) AS correct_total
FROM orders;
Expected comparison: incorrect total = 98,043.00; correct total = 52,746.00. These totals mix INR and USD and therefore should not be published as a single monetary figure; currency must be included in the grouping.
Practice 2

Produce a valid order-total summary

5. Keys identify records and protect relationships

A primary key uniquely identifies each row. For example, customer.customer_id distinguishes Aditi Sharma from every other customer even if another person has the same name.

A candidate key is a minimal attribute, or minimal combination of attributes, that could uniquely identify a row. Customer ID and email may both qualify. The candidate chosen as the primary key becomes the primary key; another candidate becomes an alternate key.

A business key has meaning outside the database, such as an email address, SKU, promotion code, or shipment tracking number. A surrogate key is a system-created identifier without direct business meaning. Gold dimensions often use surrogate keys because business attributes can change while historical identity must remain stable.

A composite key combines columns. The primary key of order_promotion is (order_id, promotion_id), preventing the same promotion from being attached twice to the same order.

A foreign key requires the referenced parent record to exist. This stops an order from being assigned to customer 999 when customer 999 is absent, protecting customer order history and downstream segmentation.

KeyEcomSphere exampleWhy the business depends on it
Primary keyorders.order_idPrevents two stored orders from sharing one identity.
Candidate keycustomer.emailCould uniquely identify a customer, but it may change.
Alternate keycustomer.email after customer ID is selectedPreserves an additional business uniqueness rule.
Business keyproduct_variant.skuKeeps fulfilment and catalog systems aligned to the same sellable item.
Foreign keyorders.customer_idPrevents an order from being disconnected from its customer.
Composite key(order_id, promotion_id)Prevents duplicate use of one promotion on the same order.

6. Build relationships from business sentences

Start with the sentence: Customer places Order. Customer and Order are the participating entities. Places is the relationship type because it names the association.

TermPlain meaningEcomSphere example
Relationship typeThe kind of associationCustomer places Order
Relationship instanceOne actual connectionAditi Sharma places order 1001
Relationship setAll current connections of that typeAll stored customer-order links
Relationship attributeA detail about the connection itselfdiscount_amount in order_promotion

Degree: how many entity types take part?

Relationship degree counts entity types, not rows. Most EcomSphere relationships are binary because two entity types take part.

DegreeMeaningExample
UnaryOne entity type relates to itselfCategory contains subcategory
BinaryTwo entity types take partCustomer places Order
TernaryThree entity types take partDoctor prescribes Medicine to Patient

The course will use binary relationships in detail. Unary and ternary relationships are introduced here so that their names are familiar when they appear in larger models.

Maximum relationship size

The maximum can create one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many relationships. Choosing the wrong maximum changes what the system can store.

  • One-to-one: one record relates to at most one record on the other side.
  • One-to-many: one parent can relate to many children. One customer can place many orders.
  • Many-to-many: both sides can have many related records. Orders and promotions follow this pattern.
Demonstration - Observe one customer with many orders 
SELECT
    c.customer_id,
    c.full_name,
    COUNT(o.order_id) AS order_count
FROM customer AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o
  ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
GROUP BY c.customer_id, c.full_name
ORDER BY c.customer_id;

The LEFT JOIN retains Ethan Miller even though he has no orders. Losing zero-activity customers would make customer-base and conversion analysis incomplete.

Turn many-to-many into two one-to-many relationships

One order may use several promotions, and one promotion may be used on several orders. MySQL resolves this through order_promotion. Its discount_amount is a relationship attribute: it describes how much one promotion reduced one order.

Demonstration - Traverse a many-to-many relationship 
SELECT
    o.order_id,
    p.promotion_code,
    op.discount_amount
FROM orders AS o
JOIN order_promotion AS op
  ON o.order_id = op.order_id
JOIN promotion AS p
  ON op.promotion_id = p.promotion_id
ORDER BY o.order_id, p.promotion_code;
Observe: order 1001 has two promotions, so two relationship records are required.

7. Add minimum and maximum constraints to the ERD

Every relationship needs two limits. The minimum says whether zero related records are allowed. The maximum says whether the limit is one or many.

Part of the ruleCourse termAllowed valuesPlain question
MinimumOrdinality or optionality0 or 1Can this record exist without the relationship?
MaximumCardinality1 or manyHow many related records are allowed at most?
Minimum labelParticipation constraintPartial or totalIs participation optional or mandatory?

Minimum 0 means optional or partial participation. Minimum 1 means mandatory or total participation. These are different names for the same minimum rule, so they should be learned together.

Course convention: this lesson uses cardinality for the maximum and ordinality for the minimum. Some books use “cardinality” for the complete min–max pair. Always check the notation being used.

Four min–max combinations

Min–maxMeaningSimple wording
0..1Optional, at most oneMay have one
1..1Mandatory, exactly oneMust have one
0..NOptional, many allowedMay have many
1..NMandatory, many allowedMust have at least one

The diagram now adds Crow's Foot-style min–max symbols. Read the symbol near an entity to learn how many instances of that entity can be related to one instance on the other side.

Apply min–max rules to EcomSphere

Business ruleMinimumMaximumMeaning
A customer may save addresses0ManyA newly registered customer can exist before saving an address.
An order belongs to a customer11An orphan order would make ownership and support resolution unreliable.
An order contains items1ManyAn order with no items has no sellable content.

Model rule versus database rule: orders.customer_id NOT NULL plus a foreign key ensures that every order has a valid customer. The foreign key does not ensure that every customer has an order, or that every order already has an item. Some parent-side minimum rules need transaction logic, application logic, or an additional database control.
Demonstration - Observe optional and repeated payment attempts 
SELECT
    o.order_id,
    o.status_code AS order_status,
    COUNT(p.payment_id) AS payment_attempts,
    COALESCE(
      GROUP_CONCAT(p.status_code ORDER BY p.attempted_at SEPARATOR ' -> '),
      'NO PAYMENT ATTEMPT'
    ) AS payment_journey
FROM orders AS o
LEFT JOIN payment AS p
  ON o.order_id = p.order_id
GROUP BY o.order_id, o.status_code
ORDER BY o.order_id;
Expected pattern: order 1001 has FAILED → SUCCESS, order 1002 has no payment attempt, and order 1003 has SUCCESS.

Keeping every attempt supports reconciliation and payment-failure analysis. Storing only the final success would hide gateway problems and retry behaviour.

Practice 4

Interpret customer-address participation

Write a query that returns every customer and their number of saved addresses, including customers with none. Then state the minimum and maximum number of addresses allowed by the sample model.

Show solution 
SELECT
    c.customer_id,
    c.full_name,

8. Move from a simple ERD to a physical MySQL EER diagram

A model can be shown at three levels. Start with a simple business picture and add detail only when it is needed. This makes a large model easier to read.

Conceptual

Shows major business entities and high-level relationships. A product owner can confirm that Customer places Order without reviewing SQL types.

Logical

Adds attributes, keys, min–max constraints, and business rules while remaining mostly platform-neutral.

Physical

Defines MySQL tables, column names, data types, constraints, indexes, and implementation details that the database executes.

Third ER diagram: show the MySQL implementation

The physical diagram shows table and column names, keys, and data types. MySQL Workbench calls this an Enhanced Entity-Relationship diagram, or EER diagram.

Hands-on - Generate the EER diagram in MySQL Workbench

  2. In MySQL Workbench, select Database → Reverse Engineer.
  3. Select the same MySQL connection and continue after the connection check succeeds.
  4. Select the ecomsphere_modelling schema.
  5. On the object-selection page, keep Place imported objects on a diagram selected.
  6. For a readable first diagram, select customer, customer_address, orders, order_item, payment, promotion, and order_promotion.
  7. Complete the wizard and arrange the tables so that relationship lines do not cross table names.
  8. Save the model as an .mwb file if you want to reopen it later.

9. Operational and analytical models solve different problems

EcomSphere's source model is an OLTP model: it supports small, frequent transactions such as registering a customer, placing an order, retrying a payment, reserving inventory, and creating a shipment. Separating related entities reduces unnecessary duplication and makes individual updates safer.

Analytics asks broader questions: monthly revenue, payment failure rate, product-category performance, repeat customers, inventory availability, and delivery delays. Answering them directly from many normalized source tables is possible, but every report must repeat complex joins and consistent business rules.

AreaOperational modelAnalytical model
Primary purposeRun business transactionsMeasure and explain business performance

    currency_code,

10. Integrated EcomSphere challenge

EcomSphere wants a customer-order summary showing every customer, number of orders, and total value split by currency. Customers with no orders must remain visible because the marketing team uses the result to distinguish purchasers from registered non-purchasers.

Practice 6

Build the customer-order summary

Create a query using customer and orders. Keep all customers, group monetary values by currency, and avoid joining order_item because the requested measure is stored at order grain.

  • Return customer ID, name, currency, order count, and order value.
  • Use NO_ORDER_CURRENCY for customers without orders.
  • Return zero instead of null for their order value.
Show solution 
SELECT
    c.customer_id,
    c.full_name,
    COALESCE(o.currency_code, 'NO_ORDER_CURRENCY') AS currency_code,
    COUNT(o.order_id) AS order_count,
    COALESCE(SUM(o.grand_total), 0.00) AS total_order_value
FROM customer AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o
  ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
GROUP BY
    c.customer_id,
    c.full_name,
    COALESCE(o.currency_code, 'NO_ORDER_CURRENCY')
ORDER BY c.customer_id, currency_code;

The left join retains Ethan Miller, who has no order. Grouping by currency prevents unlike monetary units from being added. Staying at order grain prevents order totals from being repeated by item rows.

11. Common mistakes and better choices

MistakeBetter choiceConsequence avoided
Start by listing columns without business rulesIdentify the process, entities, and relationships firstImportant events such as payment retries are not lost.
Assume every column ending in _id is a primary keyIdentify whether it is primary, foreign, business, or part of a composite keyRelationships and uniqueness rules stay accurate.
Use a changing email as the customer primary keyUse a stable customer ID and keep email uniqueCustomer history survives email changes.
Store several promotion IDs in one text columnUse order_promotionPromotions remain queryable and discount amounts stay attributable.
Draw only 1:M and ignore the minimumAdd 0..N, 1..N, or another complete min–max ruleThe model shows whether participation is optional or mandatory.
Put every column into the first ER diagramMove from conceptual to logical to physical detailNew readers can understand the business before reading implementation details.
Sum an order measure after joining to itemsAggregate at the measure's declared grainRevenue is not multiplied by item count.
Use an inner join when zero related records matterUse a left join where business questions require complete parent coverageCustomers with no address, order, or payment are not silently removed.

12. Quick knowledge check

Answer each question before opening its answer.

1. What is the difference between an entity and a table?

An entity is a business object, event, or concept. A table is a physical database structure used to store records about it.

2. What does one row in order_item represent?

One product-variant line included in an order. This is order-item grain, not order grain.

3. Why is customer_id preferred over email as the primary key?

customer_id is stable and has no changing business meaning. Email can change, although it should still be governed as a unique business attribute.

4. Which relationship exists between Order and Payment?

One-to-many. One order may have zero or many payment attempts, while each payment attempt belongs to one order.

5. Why is order_promotion required?

It resolves the many-to-many relationship between orders and promotions and stores the discount amount contributed by each relationship.

6. What is the difference between cardinality and ordinality?

In this course, cardinality gives the maximum, such as one or many. Ordinality gives the minimum, such as zero or one. Optionality is another way to discuss the same minimum rule.

7. How are ordinality and participation constraints connected?

Minimum 0 means optional or partial participation. Minimum 1 means mandatory or total participation.

8. What is relationship degree?

Degree is the number of entity types taking part. Customer places Order is binary because two entity types participate.

9. Which model contains DECIMAL(12,2) and FOREIGN KEY syntax?

The physical model, because those are database implementation details.

10. Why did joining orders to order_item inflate the order total?

The order-level total repeated once for each matching item-level row. The measure and the joined rows had different grains.

11. Why should INR and USD totals not be added directly?

They are different units of measure. Values must remain grouped by currency or be converted with a governed exchange-rate rule.

12. Why will the Gold model differ from the operational source model?

The operational model is optimized for safe day-to-day transactions. Gold models are organized around consistent analytical questions, measures, filtering, grouping, and reporting performance.

13. Session summary

  • Data modelling turns business rules into structures that engineers and databases can enforce.
  • Entities describe business objects or events; attributes describe their characteristics.
  • Every table needs a clear one-row meaning, called its grain.
  • Primary keys identify rows, while foreign keys connect valid parent and child records.
  • Business keys carry business meaning; surrogate keys provide stable system identity.
  • One-to-many and many-to-many relationships must reflect actual business behaviour.
  • Relationship degree counts participating entity types.
  • Cardinality describes the maximum; ordinality or optionality describes the minimum.
  • Minimum 0 means partial participation; minimum 1 means total participation.
  • ER diagrams can progress from a business view to a complete MySQL EER implementation.
  • Conceptual, logical, and physical models add progressively more detail.
  • Operational models support transactions; Gold models support governed analytics.
  • Grain, currency, and join choices directly affect whether financial results are correct.
